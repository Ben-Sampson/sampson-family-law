Co-Parenting Through the Holidays

Practical ways separated parents can reduce conflict and protect children's joy during the busiest season of the year.

By the Sampson Family Law editorial team (fictional) · 4 min read · General information, not legal advice.

Holidays magnify everything about co-parenting: the logistics, the emotions, and the stakes for children who want to enjoy the season without feeling caught in the middle.

Read your parenting plan early

Many disputes start because someone assumed the schedule rather than checking it. Review holiday provisions weeks in advance, confirm exchange times in writing, and clarify any ambiguity calmly before plans are made.

Put the child's experience first

Children remember how the holidays felt more than exactly where they spent each day. Avoid scheduling that turns the season into a series of rushed handoffs, and resist the urge to compete with gifts.

Communicate like colleagues

Keep messages brief, factual, and polite. Co-parenting apps can help by keeping a clear record and reducing the temptation to relitigate old arguments.

Know when to get help

If the other parent repeatedly ignores the schedule, or if there are safety concerns, document what happens and talk to an attorney about enforcement or modification options.

The best holiday gift for a child of separated parents is permission to enjoy both homes.
