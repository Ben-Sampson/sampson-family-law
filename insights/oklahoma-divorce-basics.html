Divorce in Oklahoma: Grounds, Timing, Property, and Alimony

How Oklahoma law handles grounds, residency, waiting periods, property division, and alimony, with the statutes and cases behind each rule.

By the Sampson Family Law editorial team (fictional) · 8 min read · General information, not legal advice.

A divorce in Oklahoma follows a fairly predictable statutory path. Knowing the basic rules on grounds, timing, property, and support makes it easier to plan and to ask good questions at your first meeting. The cases and statutes cited below are listed with links at the end of this article.

Grounds: most divorces are "no-fault"

Oklahoma recognizes twelve grounds for divorce, including adultery, abandonment for one year, and extreme cruelty. Most people file on incompatibility, which does not require either spouse to prove wrongdoing (43 O.S. § 101).

Residency

Either you or your spouse must have been a good-faith Oklahoma resident for the six months immediately before filing. Six months on a U.S. military post or reservation in Oklahoma also qualifies (43 O.S. § 102).

Waiting period and the parenting class

When minor children are involved, the court generally will not enter a final decree until 90 days after filing. The court can waive that for good cause if neither side objects, and some fault grounds are excluded (43 O.S. § 107.1). In an incompatibility divorce with a child under 18, both parents must complete an approved class on how divorce affects children, usually within 45 days of the temporary order. Custody will not be finalized until the class is done. The court can excuse attendance for good cause, including domestic violence (43 O.S. § 107.2(B)).

Dividing property

Oklahoma divides property "equitably," which is not necessarily equally. Each spouse keeps what he or she owned before the marriage and anything received during the marriage in his or her own right, such as gifts and inheritances. Property acquired jointly during the marriage is divided in a way that is "just and reasonable," regardless of whose name is on the title (43 O.S. § 121(B)). Property acquired during the marriage is presumed to be marital, and the spouse who claims something is separate has to prove it. Separate property can lose that status if the spouses treat it as shared (Standefer, 2001 OK 37, ¶¶ 15–16). Growth in a premarital asset, such as a retirement account, is marital only to the extent it came from contributions or effort during the marriage, not from passive market gains (Thielenhaus, 1995 OK 5, ¶ 22).

Alimony

Either spouse may be awarded alimony the court finds reasonable (43 O.S. § 121(B)). Alimony is "awarded based on demonstrated need." A spouse asking for it should be ready to show what the transition will cost and how long it will take (Johnson, 1983 OK 117, ¶¶ 23–24). The decree must separate support payments, which can be modified and end on death or remarriage, from property-division payments, which cannot be modified. Support alimony can later be changed only on a substantial, continuing change in circumstances. Cohabitation by the recipient is a separate ground to seek modification (43 O.S. § 134).

What this means for you

Gather records that show what you owned before the marriage and how accounts were handled during it. Tracing often decides whether an asset is separate or shared. If support is at issue, a realistic monthly budget is the foundation of any request.

Statutes and cases

Statutes

  1. 43 O.S. § 101Lists the twelve grounds for divorce, including incompatibility (ground Seventh).
  2. 43 O.S. § 102Requires six months of good-faith Oklahoma residence before filing; military-post residence counts.
  3. 43 O.S. § 107.1Bars a final decree for 90 days after filing when minor children are involved, subject to waiver and listed exceptions.
  4. 43 O.S. § 107.2Allows court-ordered parenting education and requires it in incompatibility divorces with a child under 18. (Amended version effective Jan. 1, 2027, with no change to the parent requirements.)
  5. 43 O.S. § 121Confirms each spouse's separate property, authorizes alimony, and requires a just and reasonable division of jointly acquired property.
  6. 43 O.S. § 134Separates support from property-division payments; ends support on death or remarriage; allows modification on substantial, continuing change or cohabitation.

Cases

  1. Thielenhaus v. Thielenhaus, 1995 OK 5, ¶ 22, 890 P.2d 925Okla. 1995. Growth in a premarital retirement fund is divisible only to the extent it results from contributions, skill, or effort during the marriage; passive appreciation remains separate.
  2. Standefer v. Standefer, 2001 OK 37, ¶¶ 15–16, 26 P.3d 104Okla. 2001. Property acquired during the marriage is presumed jointly acquired, and the party claiming separate property bears the burden; spouses who pooled personal-injury claims into a joint settlement made it marital property.
  3. Johnson v. Johnson, 1983 OK 117, ¶¶ 23–24, 674 P.2d 539Okla. 1983. Alimony is awarded based on demonstrated need; where substantial property is awarded, the claimant must prove excess needs during the readjustment period.
  4. Garcia v. Garcia, 2012 OK 81, ¶¶ 4–5, 15, 288 P.3d 931Okla. 2012. Applies § 134(D) to support-alimony modification; a resignation under threat of termination is not a voluntary, bad-faith reduction in income.
