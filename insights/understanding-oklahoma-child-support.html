Understanding Child Support in Oklahoma

How Oklahoma's guidelines calculate support, what gets added or adjusted, and how orders are modified and enforced.

By the Sampson Family Law editorial team (fictional) · 9 min read · General information, not legal advice.

Child support often feels like a number produced by a computer. In fact, Oklahoma's guidelines follow a clear statutory framework, and knowing it helps parents spot errors and plan ahead. Citations are listed with links at the end of this article.

How the basic number is calculated

The guidelines create a rebuttable presumption that the formula amount is correct (43 O.S. § 118). Both parents' adjusted gross incomes are added together, and the statutory schedule gives a base obligation for that combined income and number of children. That base amount is then divided between the parents in proportion to their incomes. This approach is commonly called the "income shares" model (43 O.S. §§ 118D, 119). The same calculation applies whether custody is labeled sole or joint (43 O.S. § 118D(E)).

What counts as income

Gross income includes wages, bonuses, commissions, and most passive income. Means-tested benefits such as TANF and SSI are excluded. If a parent is willfully or voluntarily underemployed, the court may impute income based on factors such as work history, local wages, and lifestyle. Self-employment income is calculated after ordinary business expenses, but accelerated depreciation is not deducted (43 O.S. § 118B).

Health insurance and child care

Every order must address health-care coverage. Coverage is generally "reasonable in cost" if the child's share of the premium is no more than 5% of the responsible parent's gross income, and "accessible" if suitable providers are within 60 miles (43 O.S. § 118F). Work-related child care, and child care needed for a job search or for school, is shared in proportion to income and added to support (43 O.S. § 118G).

Shared parenting time

If the parent who pays support has the children for at least 121 overnights a year, a statutory adjustment may reduce support. The adjustment is presumptive, not automatic. If a parent consistently does not use those overnights, that is a material change that can undo the adjustment (43 O.S. § 118E).

Departing from the guidelines

A court may set a different amount only if doing so is in the child's best interests and the guideline amount is unjust or inappropriate, or the parties have agreed under the conditions the statute specifies. The court must make written findings, and no deviation may leave the child without basic necessities (43 O.S. § 118H).

How long support lasts

Support generally continues until age 18. If the child is still a full-time high school student, it continues until graduation or age 20, whichever comes first (43 O.S. § 112(E)). Support for a child whose disability began before age 18 may continue indefinitely (43 O.S. § 112.1A).

Changing an order

Support can be modified when there is a material change in circumstances, such as a change in income or in the child's needs. A modification generally takes effect the first day of the month after the motion is filed and cannot be applied retroactively, so waiting can be costly (43 O.S. § 118I). When income drops because of a parent's own conduct, the court looks at whether the parent acted in good or bad faith (Garcia, 2012 OK 81, ¶ 15).

Enforcement

Each unpaid installment becomes a judgment by operation of law on its due date. These judgments do not go dormant and remain enforceable until paid. Willful nonpayment can also be addressed through contempt (43 O.S. § 137). Social Security benefits paid to a child because of a parent's disability may be credited against that parent's obligation (43 O.S. § 118B(F); Merritt, 2003 OK 68, ¶¶ 18–19).

Oklahoma Human Services operates a child support services program that can help establish, modify, and enforce orders. National collection data is summarized on our evidence page.

Statutes and cases

Statutes

  1. 43 O.S. § 112Subsection E: support continues to age 18, or to high school graduation or age 20 (whichever is first) for a full-time student.
  2. 43 O.S. § 112.1AAuthorizes indefinite support for an adult child with a disability that existed before age 18.
  3. 43 O.S. § 118Makes the guideline amount the rebuttably presumed correct amount of support.
  4. 43 O.S. § 118ADefines key terms, including adjusted gross income, custodial person, and overnight.
  5. 43 O.S. § 118BDefines gross income, exclusions, imputed income, self-employment income, and Social Security/VA benefit credits.
  6. 43 O.S. § 118CProvides deductions for other children a parent supports.
  7. 43 O.S. § 118DComputes support from combined income and allocates it in proportion to each parent's income, for sole or joint custody alike.
  8. 43 O.S. § 118EProvides the parenting-time adjustment at 121 or more overnights per year.
  9. 43 O.S. § 118FRequires medical support orders; sets the 5% cost and 60-mile accessibility standards.
  10. 43 O.S. § 118GAllocates actual annualized child-care costs between the parents.
  11. 43 O.S. § 118HSets the grounds, required findings, and limits for deviations from the guidelines.
  12. 43 O.S. § 118IAllows modification on a material change in circumstances; prohibits retroactive modification; abates support during long incarceration.
  13. 43 O.S. § 119Contains the Schedule of Basic Child Support Obligations.
  14. 43 O.S. § 137Makes each past-due installment a judgment by operation of law that does not go dormant.

Cases

  1. Garcia v. Garcia, 2012 OK 81, ¶¶ 10, 15–16, 288 P.3d 931Okla. 2012. On a motion to modify after an income loss, the court must decide whether the reduction was in good or bad faith; resigning under threat of termination is not voluntary.
  2. Merritt v. Merritt, 2003 OK 68, ¶¶ 13, 18–19, 73 P.3d 878Okla. 2003. Arrearages are judgments by operation of law but remain subject to equitable considerations; Social Security disability benefits paid to the child may be credited against support.
