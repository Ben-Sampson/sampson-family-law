## PART 1: DIVORCE (DISSOLUTION OF MARRIAGE)

### 1.1 Grounds for divorce

**Plain-English summary.** Oklahoma allows divorce on any of twelve statutory grounds. Most people file on **incompatibility**, which means neither spouse has to prove fault. If you have a child under 18 and you file on incompatibility, both parents must complete a court-approved class on how divorce affects children (see 1.3).

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 101** | Lists twelve grounds, including abandonment for one year, adultery, extreme cruelty, fraudulent contract, **incompatibility** (ground "Seventh"), habitual drunkenness, gross neglect of duty, and felony imprisonment. The incompatibility ground requires the parenting-education program under § 107.2(B) when a child under 18 is involved. Last amended Laws 2014, c. 428, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71811 |

### 1.2 Residency

**Plain-English summary.** Before you file, either you or your spouse must have lived in Oklahoma in good faith for the **six months** immediately before filing. Six months on a U.S. military post or reservation in Oklahoma also counts.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 102** | (A) The petitioner *or* the respondent must have been an actual good-faith Oklahoma resident for six months immediately before the petition is filed. (B) The same six-month rule applies to residence on a U.S. Army post or military reservation in Oklahoma. Last amended Laws 2002, c. 400, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71812 |

### 1.3 Waiting period and parenting education

**Plain-English summary.** When minor children are involved, the court generally cannot enter a final decree until **90 days after filing**. The court can waive that for good cause if neither side objects, and the waiting period does not apply to certain fault grounds, such as extreme cruelty or abandonment. In an incompatibility divorce with a child under 18, both parents must take a class on how divorce affects children. The class must be completed before the temporary order or within 45 days after it. The court will not finalize custody until both parents have completed the class. The court can excuse attendance for good cause, including domestic violence.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 107.1** | (A) In divorces involving minor children, no final order for at least 90 days after filing. The court may waive this for good cause shown and without objection. The court may require the § 107.2 program during that period. (B) The rule does not apply to divorces on listed grounds (abandonment, extreme cruelty, habitual drunkenness, felony imprisonment, foreign decree, insanity, certain child-abuse convictions or deprived adjudications). (D) The court may enter the decree early if the parties went to counseling and reconciliation is unlikely. Last amended Laws 2002, c. 445, § 16. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71818 |
| **43 O.S. § 107.2** | (A) In divorce, custody, paternity, and similar actions involving a child under 18, the court *may* order an educational program for the adults. (B) In incompatibility divorces filed on or after Nov. 1, 2014 with a child under 18, attendance is *mandatory*. The statute sets out six required components and a fee of $10 to $60, which can be waived for qualifying free programs. The program must be completed before the temporary order or within 45 days of it, and custody cannot be finally decided until it is completed. Attendance can be waived for good cause, including domestic violence, stalking, or harassment. | Current: https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=553443 |

Subsections A and B for parents are unchanged. | Future version: https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71819 |

### 1.4 Property division

**Plain-English summary.** Oklahoma is an **equitable-distribution** state, not a community-property state. Each spouse keeps the property he or she owned before the marriage and property received during the marriage "in his or her own right," such as gifts and inheritances. Property "jointly acquired" during the marriage is divided in a way that is "just and reasonable." That is not always 50/50. Property acquired during the marriage is presumed to be marital, so the spouse who claims something is separate has to prove it. Separate property can lose its separate character if the spouses treat it as joint property. Growth in a separate asset during the marriage is marital only to the extent it came from either spouse's effort, skill, or contributions, not from passive market growth.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 121(B)** | The decree must confirm to each spouse the property owned before marriage and property acquired during marriage "in his or her own right." Property "acquired by the parties jointly during their marriage, whether the title thereto be in either or both" is divided as "may appear just and reasonable," subject to a valid written antenuptial contract. The court may divide property in kind or award it to one spouse and order a compensating payment. Subsections C to G cover military Special Monthly Compensation, CRSC, and the formula for dividing military retirement. Last amended Laws 2012, c. 334, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71844 |
| ***Thielenhaus v. Thielenhaus*, 1995 OK 5, ¶ 22, 890 P.2d 925** (Okla. 1995) | Growth during the marriage in a spouse's premarital retirement fund is divisible marital property if it comes from employer contributions or either spouse's money, skill, or effort. Growth from investment managed by neither spouse, or from appreciation, inflation, or market forces, stays separate. The trial court sets the valuation date. (Decided Jan. 31, 1995; modified Sept. 1, 1995.) | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/deliverdocument.asp?citeid=4119 ; text checked at https://law.justia.com/cases/oklahoma/supreme-court/1995/4119-1.html |
| ***Standefer v. Standefer*, 2001 OK 37, ¶¶ 15–16, 26 P.3d 104** (Okla. 2001) | Property acquired during marriage is presumed jointly acquired, and the spouse claiming separate property has the burden of proof (¶ 15, citing *Gray v. Gray*, 1996 OK 84, ¶ 11, 922 P.2d 615). Spouses who pooled personal-injury claims into one joint settlement, held jointly, made the proceeds marital property (¶ 16). The trial court has discretion to divide marital property in a way that is fair, just, and reasonable (¶ 19). | https://www.oscn.net/applications/OCISWeb/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=212383 |

### 1.5 Alimony (support alimony)

**Plain-English summary.** Either spouse can be awarded alimony when the court considers it reasonable. Oklahoma separates **support alimony**, which is modifiable and ends at the recipient's death or remarriage, from **property-division payments**, which are not modifiable. The decree must state which part of each payment is which. Alimony depends on demonstrated **need**, and the spouse asking for it should be ready to prove what it will cost to get back on their feet financially and how long that will take. Support alimony can later be changed if there is a substantial, continuing change in need or ability to pay. Cohabitation of the recipient can also be a ground for modification.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 121(B)** | Either spouse "may be allowed such alimony out of real and personal property of the other as the court shall think reasonable," payable in a lump sum or in installments. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71844 |
| **43 O.S. § 134** | (A) The decree must separately state the support and property-division parts of periodic payments. Property-division payments are irrevocable and cannot be modified. (B) Support ends on the recipient's death or remarriage. After remarriage, the recipient has 90 days to show continued need. (C) Voluntary cohabitation is a ground to modify support. (D) Support can be modified prospectively for "substantial and continuing" changed circumstances. (K) VA service-related disability compensation may not be considered. Last amended Laws 2017, c. 274, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71857 |
| ***Johnson v. Johnson*, 1983 OK 117, ¶¶ 23–24, 674 P.2d 539** (Okla. 1983) | "Alimony is awarded based on demonstrated need." Where a spouse receives substantial property, an alimony claim must be supported by proof of excess monetary needs during the transition to self-support: the cost of maintenance during readjustment, the income from the property awarded, and the time needed. | https://oscn.net/applications/OCISWeb/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=9572 ; https://law.justia.com/cases/oklahoma/supreme-court/1983/9572.html |
| ***Garcia v. Garcia*, 2012 OK 81, ¶¶ 4–5, 15, 288 P.3d 931** (Okla. 2012) | Applies § 134(D) to support-alimony modification and § 118I to child support. A coerced resignation (resign or be fired) is not a voluntary income reduction. Courts must decide whether a reduction in income was in good or bad faith (that is, done to avoid support) before refusing to modify. (Also listed under 2.5.) | https://oscn.net/applications/OCISWeb/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=468236 |

### 1.6 Custody: best interests, joint custody, and mandatory considerations

**Plain-English summary.** Custody is decided based on the child's best interests, meaning "the physical and mental and moral welfare of the child." Courts can award custody to either parent or jointly. A parent who asks for joint custody must file a joint-custody plan. Since **Nov. 1, 2024**, a court finding of child abuse, domestic violence (expressly including coercive control), stalking, or harassment creates a rebuttable presumption that custody with the abusive parent is not in the child's best interests. Courts must also consider specific risk factors, such as sex-offender registration or certain criminal convictions, and some of those factors create rebuttable presumptions against custody. Changing an existing custody order requires a showing of a permanent, substantial, and material change in circumstances.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 109** | (A) Best interests of "the physical and mental and moral welfare of the child." (B) Custody may go to either parent or jointly. Joint custody is defined. (C)–(G) Joint-custody plans, affidavits, modification, and termination. (H) Arbitration of plan disputes. (I) A finding of child abuse, domestic violence (including coercive control), stalking, or harassment creates a rebuttable presumption against custody with the perpetrator. The victim's safety is "a primary factor." **Amended Laws 2024, SB 901, c. 24, § 1, eff. Nov. 1, 2024.** | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71822 |
| **43 O.S. § 112.2** | (A) In every custody, guardianship, or visitation case, the court must consider whether a party (or someone living with that party) is a registered sex offender, has been convicted of listed child-abuse crimes or domestic abuse within 5 years, or is alcohol- or drug-dependent as defined. (B) Some of these factors create a rebuttable presumption against custody. **Amended Laws 2024, SB 1756, c. 34, § 2, eff. Nov. 1, 2024.** | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=439786 |
| **43 O.S. § 112** | The general statute on care, custody, and support of minor children. Subsection E sets the duration of child support (see 2.6). | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71829 |
| ***Gibbons v. Gibbons*, 1968 OK 77, ¶ 12, 442 P.2d 482** (Okla. 1968) | The parent seeking to change custody must prove (a) a permanent, substantial, and material change of conditions that directly affects the child's best interests since the last order, and (b) that the child would be substantially better off, temporally, mentally, and morally, with the change. This is still the governing modification test. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/OCISWeb/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=41548 |

### 1.7 Relocation

**Plain-English summary.** A parent who wants to move a child's principal residence **more than 75 miles for 60 days or more** must give written notice by mail at least **60 days** before the move. The notice must include the new address, the reasons for the move, a proposed new visitation schedule, and a warning about the 30-day objection deadline. The other parent has **30 days** after receiving notice to file an objection, or the move is allowed. If there is an objection, the moving parent must first show the move is in **good faith**. The burden then shifts to the objecting parent to show the move is **not** in the child's best interests. The court weighs eight statutory factors.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 112.3** | Defines "relocation" (over 75 miles, 60+ days) (A)(5). Requires 60-day mailed notice with listed contents (C). Requires a standard notice paragraph in custody orders (D). Sets a 30-day objection window (G). Allows temporary orders (H). Lists eight best-interest factors (J). Burden-shifting: the relocating person proves good faith, then the nonrelocating person must show the move is not in the child's best interest (K). Allows sanctions for bad-faith proposals or objections (L). Last amended Laws 2008, c. 28, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=388969 |
| ***Mahmoodjanloo v. Mahmoodjanloo*, 2007 OK 32, ¶ 12, 160 P.3d 951** (Okla. 2007) | § 112.3(K) "plainly and unambiguously" puts the burden on the relocating parent only to show good faith. The burden then shifts to the objecting parent to show the move is not in the children's best interest. The trial court erred by also requiring the relocating father to prove the move was in the children's best interest. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/OCISWeb/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=449033 |
| ***Kaiser v. Kaiser*, 2001 OK 30, ¶¶ 18, 33, 23 P.3d 278** (Okla. 2001) | Decided before § 112.3. A custodial parent has a statutory presumptive right to relocate (then 10 O.S. § 19, now renumbered 43 O.S. § 112.2A) absent prejudice to the child (¶ 18). A move is not by itself a change of circumstances justifying a custody change, and the dispositive issue is fitness and risk of "specific and real harm" (¶ 33). Altered visitation alone does not justify restricting a move (¶ 29). | https://oscn.net/applications/OCISWeb/deliverdocument.asp?citeid=205426 ; https://law.justia.com/cases/oklahoma/supreme-court/2001/205426.html |
| ***Scocos v. Scocos*, 2016 OK 36, ¶ 6, 369 P.3d 1068** (Okla. 2016) | Reads § 112.2A and § 112.3 together. Under § 112.3(K), the relocating parent must prove good faith, which the court defined using 25 O.S. § 9 as "an honest intention to abstain from taking any unconscientious advantage of another." **[PARTLY VERIFIED: ¶ 6 text and the cite were confirmed from Justia and from OSCN's citing-reference table. The full opinion was not opened on OSCN.]** | https://law.justia.com/cases/oklahoma/supreme-court/2016/112723.html |

## PART 2: CHILD SUPPORT

### 2.1 The guidelines framework

**Plain-English summary.** Oklahoma calculates child support by adding both parents' adjusted gross incomes, finding the base support amount on the statutory schedule for that combined income and number of children, and then dividing that amount between the parents in proportion to their incomes. This is commonly called the **income-shares** model. The guideline result is presumed correct, but either parent can try to rebut it. The same calculation applies whether the custody arrangement is labeled sole or joint. Health-insurance premiums and work-related child care are then added and shared in the same proportion.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 118** | (A) Creates a rebuttable presumption that the guideline amount is correct in any judicial or administrative proceeding. (B) The schedule assumes average child-rearing costs (housing, food, transportation, basic public education, clothing, entertainment). Last amended Laws 2008, c. 407, § 1, eff. July 1, 2009. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=439676 |
| **43 O.S. § 118A** | Definitions: Adjusted Gross Income (AGI); base child support obligation (presumptively correct); current monthly obligation; "custodial person" (more than 182 days a year); "overnight" (at least 12 hours plus a reasonable expenditure of resources); incarceration; and others. Last amended Laws 2021, c. 286, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452143 |
| **43 O.S. § 118B** | Gross income: earned and passive income, listed exclusions such as TANF, SSI, and child support received for other children. Use the "most equitable" of current, prior-year average, or imputed income. Imputation factors include willful or voluntary underemployment, lifestyle, and minimum wage at 25 hours or more per week. Self-employment income excludes accelerated depreciation. Fringe benefits. Social Security and VA benefits paid to the child are credited. Last amended Laws 2021, c. 286, § 2. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452144 |

Rules for split custody (D). Same computation "regardless of whether the custodial arrangement is designated as sole custody or joint custody" (E). Transportation costs may be shared (G). | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452146 |
| **43 O.S. § 119** | The Schedule of Basic Child Support Obligations, for combined monthly income up to $15,000. ### 2.2 Health insurance (medical support)

**Plain-English summary.** Every child support order must address health-care coverage. Coverage is "reasonable in cost" if the child's share of the premium is no more than **5% of the responsible parent's gross income**. It is "accessible" if appropriate providers are within **60 miles** of the child's home. The child's premium is shared in proportion to the parents' incomes and added to base support.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 118F** | Medical support orders are required in every ongoing support order (A). Temporary orders continue existing coverage (B). Defines the 5%-of-gross-income cost standard and the 60-mile accessibility standard (D). The custodial person's preference gets priority if both parents have coverage (E). Cash medical support (F–G). Premiums are allocated pro rata by AGI (H). Last amended Laws 2018, c. 87, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452148 |

### 2.3 Child care

**Plain-English summary.** Reasonable child-care costs needed so a parent can work, look for work, or attend school or training are annualized, shared in proportion to income, and added to the support order. The parent paying for child care must tell the other parent about changes within 45 days.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 118G** | Actual annualized child-care costs for employment, job search, or school or training (A). Uses the DHS subsidy copay schedule when applicable (B). Costs are allocated and added to base support (C). 45-day notice of changes (D). Last amended Laws 2021, c. 286, § 3. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452149 |

### 2.4 Shared-parenting (parenting-time) adjustment

**Plain-English summary.** If the parent who pays support has the children for **at least 121 overnights a year**, support may be reduced through a statutory formula. The base obligation is multiplied by 2.0 at 121–131 overnights, by 1.75 at 132–143, and by 1.5 at 144 or more. Each parent's share is then cross-multiplied by the other parent's percentage of time, and the two amounts are offset. The adjustment is presumptive but can be rebutted. If a parent does not actually use the overnights, that is a material change in circumstances, and the underpaid amount can become a judgment.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 118E** | Eligibility at **121 or more overnights** per 12-month period, with averaging across multiple children (A, D). The adjustment is presumptive, not mandatory (C). Formula factors 2 / 1.75 / 1.5 (D)(2). Offset method (D)(4). No support is paid by a parent with more than 205 overnights (D)(5). Failure to exercise the overnights is a material change, may create a judgment, and can mean the adjustment is lost for 12 months (E). Last amended Laws 2015, c. 238, § 1. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452147 |

**Correction to the original brief:** the threshold is **121** overnights ("at least one hundred twenty-one (121)"), not "about 120."

### 2.5 Deviations from the guidelines

**Plain-English summary.** A court can set support above or below the guideline amount only if the deviation is in the child's best interests **and** one of these applies: (a) the guideline amount would be unjust or inappropriate, (b) both parties have lawyers and agreed to it, or (c) one party has a lawyer and the deviation benefits the unrepresented party. The court must make written findings. No deviation may leave the receiving parent unable to provide basic housing, food, and clothing. Special expenses such as private school tuition, camps, lessons, and extracurricular activities can be added as deviations.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 118H** | Floor: no deviation that seriously impairs minimally adequate housing, food, and clothing (A). Grounds for deviation (B). Required specific findings, including what the guideline amount would have been (C). Extreme economic hardship (D). Children in DHS custody (F). Extraordinary educational expenses (G). Special expenses (H). | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452150 |

### 2.6 Duration (how long support lasts)

**Plain-English summary.** Child support generally continues until the child turns **18**. If the child is still a full-time high school student (including alternative or other recognized high-school programs), support continues until graduation or **age 20**, whichever comes first, without a new hearing. For a child with a disability that began before age 18 and that leaves the child unable to support themselves, the court may order support indefinitely.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 112(E)** | Support continues to age 18. It extends to high-school graduation or age 20, whichever is first, while the child is regularly enrolled full-time. No hearing is required for the extension. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71829 |
| **43 O.S. § 112.1A** | Allows support for an adult child for an indefinite period if the child requires substantial care because of a mental or physical disability, will not be capable of self-support, and the disability (or its cause) existed by the 18th birthday. Laws 2001, c. 407, § 5. **[Correction to the original brief: § 112.1A is the disability-support statute. The general age rule is in § 112(E).]** | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=274981 |

### 2.7 Modification

**Plain-English summary.** Support can be changed when there has been a **material change in circumstances**, such as a change in either parent's income, the child's needs, child-care or insurance costs, or a child aging out. A change to the guideline schedule alone is not enough. Changes take effect the first day of the month **after the motion is filed**, and support **cannot be changed retroactively**. That is why it matters to file promptly. Since Nov. 1, 2021, support is presumptively suspended during incarceration of 180 days or more, with exceptions, for example where the incarceration was for nonpayment of support.

|---|---|---|

No retroactive modification (B)(1). A child aging out is a material change but does not automatically reduce the order. Support for the last child ends automatically (C). Informal review and agreed modifications (D). Incarceration abatement presumption (E). Last amended Laws 2021, SB 421, c. 286, § 4. | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=452151 |

Resigning in lieu of termination is coerced, not voluntary. Denial of modification was reversed. | https://oscn.net/applications/OCISWeb/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=468236 |

### 2.8 Enforcement

**Plain-English summary.** Every missed child support payment automatically becomes a **judgment** on the date it is due. It can be collected the same ways as any other money judgment, it never becomes dormant, and it is enforceable until paid in full. Willful nonpayment can also be pursued through contempt. A payment plan for arrears is generally limited to three years unless the court makes specific findings.

| Authority | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **43 O.S. § 137** | Each past-due installment is "a judgment by operation of law," entitled to full faith and credit (A). Not dormant, enforceable until paid in full, and indirect civil contempt is still available. The lien on real property lapses after 5 years unless extended (B). Arrearage payment schedule limited to 3 years absent findings (C). | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71860 |
| **43 O.S. § 139 / § 139.1** | License revocation or suspension for noncompliance with a support order. **[UNVERIFIED: section titles confirmed from the OSCN Title 43 index only. The text was not reviewed. CiteIDs are 71862 and 440223.]** | https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/DeliverDocument.asp?CiteID=71862 |
| ***Merritt v. Merritt*, 2003 OK 68, ¶¶ 13, 18–19, 73 P.3d 878** (Okla. 2003) | Even though support arrearages are judgments by operation of law under § 137, they remain subject to equitable considerations (¶ 13). Social Security disability benefits paid to the child on the obligor parent's account may be credited against support, and a disabled parent will not be required "to pay a second time" (¶ 19).
