Criminal Defense

Defense for criminal charges in Tulsa County and across Oklahoma, from a first DUI to a felony case.

What we handle

The firm represents people facing criminal charges in Oklahoma. The practice covers driving under the influence and actual physical control, misdemeanors, felonies, traffic citations and outstanding warrants, and, where a person may qualify, petitions to seal a criminal record.

Under Oklahoma law, a person may be charged with driving under the influence for driving, operating, or being in actual physical control of a motor vehicle while impaired, or at or above the legal blood- or breath-alcohol limit (47 O.S. § 11-902). A DUI or APC arrest can start two matters at once: the criminal case in district court and a separate driver's license proceeding, each with its own rules and timelines.

How a criminal case typically moves

Most cases follow a familiar path, though the details depend on the charge and the court. After an arrest or a citation, a first appearance or arraignment is held, the charge is read, and a plea is entered. The State then provides discovery: police reports, videos, test results, and other evidence. Counsel reviews that material, raises any appropriate challenges, and discusses options, which may include a negotiated plea or a trial.

Throughout the case, you have the right to remain silent and the right to a lawyer. You do not have to explain what happened to the police, and you can ask for an attorney before answering questions. Public defenders are available for those who qualify.

What to bring to a consultation

A first meeting is more useful if you bring what you already have: the citation or charging documents, any bond paperwork, a notice about your driver's license if one was issued, and a list of upcoming court dates. If you have already spoken with another attorney about the same matter, mention that at the outset.

We will go over the charge, the next court date, and what to expect, and we will answer your questions in plain terms.

A criminal charge is its own case, with its own court, rules, and deadlines. It deserves a defense that treats it that way.

FAQ

Criminal Defense questions, answered

Should I talk to the police?

You have the right to remain silent and the right to a lawyer. You can decline to answer questions and ask for an attorney before giving a statement.

What happens after a DUI arrest?

A DUI or APC arrest can involve both a criminal case and a separate license matter. Timelines on the license side can be short, so it is worth talking with counsel quickly.

Do you handle felonies as well as misdemeanors?

Yes. The firm defends misdemeanors and felonies, as well as traffic matters, warrants, and DUI and APC cases.

Can a conviction or arrest be expunged?

In some situations, Oklahoma law allows a person to ask the court to seal a criminal record (22 O.S. § 18). Eligibility depends on the charge, the outcome, and waiting periods. A consultation is the place to review whether a petition may be available.

What should I bring to a first meeting?

Bring any citation, charging paperwork, bond or license notices, and a list of court dates. If you do not have those documents yet, come anyway and we will work with what you know.
