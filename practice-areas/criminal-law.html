Criminal Law

Coordinated defense when a criminal matter overlaps with divorce, custody, or a protective order.

Where criminal and family law meet

Family disputes and criminal charges often collide. An arrest during a domestic argument, a protective order filed alongside a divorce, or allegations raised in a custody dispute can create parallel cases in different courts with different rules, deadlines, and standards of proof.

What you say in one case can affect the other. A statement made in a family court hearing may be used in a criminal prosecution, and a plea in criminal court can shape custody decisions. Coordinating strategy across both matters is essential.

Common overlapping matters

The matters that most often overlap with family cases include domestic assault and battery charges, violations of protective orders, and child endangerment allegations. Each carries potential consequences beyond the criminal sentence, including effects on firearm rights, employment, and parenting time.

Protective orders under Oklahoma's Protection from Domestic Abuse Act are civil, but violating one can be a crime. Anyone served with an order should read it carefully and follow it exactly while the case proceeds.

Our focus

In this fictional practice, criminal work is limited to matters connected to family cases. For unrelated criminal defense, we would refer clients to experienced criminal defense counsel. Anyone facing charges should speak with a licensed criminal defense attorney promptly; public defenders are available for those who qualify.

When two courts are looking at the same family, one coordinated strategy matters more than two separate ones.

FAQ

Criminal Law questions, answered

Should I talk to the police about a family dispute?

You have the right to remain silent and to consult a lawyer. Speak with an attorney before giving statements about pending matters.

Does a protective order affect custody?

It can. Protective orders may include temporary custody and contact provisions and can be considered in later custody decisions.

Can a criminal charge be used against me in divorce?

Conduct and charges can be relevant, especially to custody. Coordinated advice helps avoid unintended consequences.

Do you handle all criminal cases?

No. In this demonstration, criminal work is limited to matters overlapping with family law.
